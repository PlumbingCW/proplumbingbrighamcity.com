August 13, 1923, and why the canyon still matters

At about 8:30 that evening, a thunderstorm sent a wall of water out of Willard Canyon. It leveled the city powerhouse, flooded fields and houses, and buried US-89 and the Utah-Idaho Central tracks in debris. The town answered with a dike and spillway built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, and later with flood-control reservoirs and pipelines.

Those works reduce the danger, but a summer cloudburst on the slopes east of town can still push mud and runoff toward low spots. Before storm season, test your sump pit, clear window wells and confirm floor drains flow. If a pit is already struggling, sump pump repair is cheaper than a flooded basement.
