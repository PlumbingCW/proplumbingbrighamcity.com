Plumber in the Brigham City Historic District, UT

The historic district is the old core of Brigham City, roughly 700 South to 500 North and 300 to 500 West out to 500 East. Call (855) 575-2890 to ask about plumbing service for a home here.

Where the district sits

The National Register of Historic Places lists the Brigham City Historic District as roughly bounded by 700 South, 500 East, 500 North, and 300 and 500 West, with an extension toward 800 West near Forest Street. It was listed on June 5, 2023. The word roughly matters, because the exact line follows individual parcels, so check your own address against the state's map before you assume you are inside.

This page is for people with a home in this part of Brigham City who want to know what to ask a plumber. It sits next to the wider plumber in Brigham City page, and the business blocks along Main Street have their own page for downtown Brigham City.

What the housing record actually shows

The district's National Register nomination counts 1,813 primary buildings, and 1,106 of them contribute to the district. Among the contributing buildings, 933 are single-family homes, which is about 84 percent. The nomination describes the district as the area where most construction predates 1950.

The same document says brick is the most common material, covering 48 percent of the primary buildings. What it does not say is what pipe is inside any wall. Age alone does not tell you that, because a house from 1925 may have had its supply lines replaced and a house from 1960 may not have. A sewer camera inspection shows the buried line, and a look at the supply pipe in the basement or crawl space shows the rest.

If a plumber tells you a whole-house replacement is needed, ask what they found and ask to see it. A repiping job should rest on evidence from your house, not on its birth year.

Plumbing points that fit homes of any age

These are general plumbing points, not a claim that homes here fail more often than homes elsewhere.

A drain that slows everywhere

If the tub, the sink and the toilet all slow at once, the blockage is usually downstream in the main line. Stop running water and read about drain cleaning before the next load of laundry.

A water heater that is losing years

Brigham City's own water page reports hardness of 17 to 20 grains per gallon. Water that hard leaves scale in a tank, so ask Public Works for the current figure, then ask about water heater replacement options and maintenance.

A shutoff valve that will not close

A valve that has not moved in years can seize. Do not force it with a wrench. Find out where the next shutoff is before you need it.

A stain you cannot trace

A damp patch or a meter that creeps with everything off points to a hidden leak. Leak detection finds the source without opening more wall than needed.

Water, Box Elder Creek and the basement

Box Elder Creek runs through Brigham City, and the district nomination notes a 1934 WPA bridge over it. Rees Pioneer Park, inside the district, began as swampy ground that was turned into a reservoir before 1900. Water has been managed here for a long time, so a basement or crawl space deserves a drain and pump that work.

A more recent record is citywide. On March 23, 2018, Box Elder Creek overflowed from debris plus snowmelt and rain, and about 10 homes had flooding, including basements. Fox13 coverage at the time does not say which streets, so do not read it as a map of this district. The practical step is to test your sump pump, if you have one, by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the pump start and discharge.

Who else to call, and when

  • The city water meter is not yours to touch. Brigham City Public Works is at (435) 734-6615, and the city lists an after-hours water emergency line at (435) 723-6868.
  • Permits and inspections go through the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604. Permit rules vary, so ask before you start a job.
  • Call 811 at least three business days before digging for a new yard line, so buried lines get marked.
  • You can check any plumber's license class with the state's public Verify a License tool.

What to have ready when you call

Give the nearest cross street and say which fixture is involved. Say where your main shutoff is if you know, and how long the problem has been going on. A photo of a stain or a valve helps. Then ask what service is available at your address and what the next step would be.
