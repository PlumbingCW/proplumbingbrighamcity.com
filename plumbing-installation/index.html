Fixtures, lines and hookups set the right way

Plumbing installation in Brigham City, UT is the work of putting in new water, drain, vent and gas connections, or hooking a fixture or appliance to the ones you already have. It is different from repair, where something that worked has stopped.

Most calls we hear fall into a few groups: a new shower or tub, a sink or toilet swap during a remodel, a washer or dryer hookup, and a property that has no plumbing run to a building yet.

Safety first

Smell gas? Leave first, then make the calls

If you smell gas, do not look for the source and do not flip a switch, light anything or use a phone inside. Leave the building right away and take everyone with you.

  1. Get outside and move away from the house.
  2. From a safe distance, call 911 or the gas utility. Enbridge Gas Utah lists a 24-hour emergency line at 1-800-767-1689.
  3. Stay out until the gas utility or fire department says it is safe.
  4. Call a plumber only after that, for repairs or a new gas line inspection.

We cannot promise how fast anyone arrives, and a plumber is never the first call for a gas smell. The utility and emergency crews have the meters and detectors for that moment.

Planning a remodel or new build in sensible order

  1. Decide fixture locations

    Pick sinks, toilets and tubs before framing, since drains and vents depend on where they sit.

  2. Rough-in

    Supply, drain and vent runs go in while walls and floors are still open.

  3. Inspection

    Rough-in work is usually inspected before walls close. Ask the building office what your job needs.

  4. Set fixtures

    After finishes go on, fixtures are set, connected and tested for leaks.

  5. Final check

    We run every fixture, check traps and shutoffs, and show you where the valves are.

Decide early, because moving a drain after the slab or floor is closed costs far more than moving it on paper.

Gas line work, once the area is safe

Calls about gas piping usually involve a stove or grill hookup, a rusty-looking line, an office or renovation that needs a new run, or a request for a clearance check after a utility visit. Some callers only want a price for an inspection.

Planned gas work starts with sizing: a run must carry enough gas for every appliance on it. We check pipe size, shutoff locations and the condition of fittings, then pressure test before anything is lit. Gas is not a place for a homeowner to experiment, and the gas utility handles anything on its side of the meter.

When a plumber is not the right call

A hot tub or jetted tub needs a dedicated electrical circuit, and wiring is an electrician's work, not ours. We handle the water and drain side.

Structural cuts through joists or foundations are a carpenter's or contractor's decision. Meter-side problems belong to Public Works at (435) 734-6615.

What shapes the price of an installation

Distance to existing lines

A fixture beside current pipes costs less than one across the house.

Fixture type

A basic toilet is simple, a body-spray shower or jetted tub is not.

Access

Open framing is quick. Finished walls or a slab add labor.

Pipe condition

Tired supply lines or valves sometimes need replacing at the same visit.

Permits and inspections

Some jobs need them, and fees and timing depend on the city.

Homeowners insurance generally covers sudden accidental water damage, not a new installation or an upgrade. Ask your agent if a failure caused the work. If supply lines behind the walls are the weak link, repiping may make more sense than a new fixture on old pipe.

Permits, licensing and the city's backflow guidance

Permit rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting. The office is at 20 N. Main, 2nd Floor. Utah adopts plumbing codes through the state, so ask which edition applies to your job.

The city publishes guidance on cross-connection control and backflow prevention. If your installation adds an irrigation hookup or a new outdoor spigot, ask Public Works what applies. You can also confirm any plumber's license class on the Utah Division of Professional Licensing Verify a License tool.

Homes in Willard, about 7 miles south, follow the same steps, and you should ask Willard City whether your address is on city sewer before planning a new drain.
