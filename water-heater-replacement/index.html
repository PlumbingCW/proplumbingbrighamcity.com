A failed tank cannot be patched

Water heater replacement means removing a worn-out tank and setting a new unit that meets the home's hot water demand. When a tank splits at the seam or the bottom rusts through, patching does not hold. Dispatch answers 24/7, and same-day installs are available when stock and the schedule allow.

Until the crew arrives, close the cold water valve on top of the heater and shut off the gas or the breaker. Slow the damage with towels and a wet vacuum if you have one, and move anything stored near the floor.

Flooded heater: the first ten minutes

  1. Turn off power at the breaker for an electric unit, or turn a gas unit's control to off.
  2. Close the cold supply valve above the heater. If it will not budge, shut the home's main.
  3. Once the cold valve is closed, open a hot tap upstairs so the tank can drain and the leak slows.
  4. Keep standing water away from outlets and the electrical panel.
  5. Take photos of the tank, the label and the water damage before you clean up.

If you smell gas while doing this, leave the house first, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside. The water can wait. The gas cannot.

Signs the tank is past saving

Some failures announce themselves, and some look like a minor drip. These are the clues that point to replacement instead of repair.

  • Water at the base that comes from the tank body, not a fitting.
  • Rust-colored water from hot taps only.
  • Heavy rumbling or popping from sediment that has hardened.
  • A tank at or past its expected service life that now needs a second repair. The date is on the rating label.
  • Corroded fittings and a pan that keeps filling after you stop the supply.

If you are not sure the tank is the problem, our water heater repair page lists the fixable failures. A good diagnosis saves you from buying a tank you do not need.

What a same-day swap looks like

  1. Shut down and drain

    We close the supply, cut power or gas and drain the tank to a floor drain or outside.

  2. Disconnect and remove

    Water lines, gas or electrical connections and the vent come off, and the old tank leaves with us.

  3. Set and level

    The new unit goes on a proper pan or base with a drain line that has somewhere to go.

  4. Reconnect with new fittings

    We use new flex connectors, a new relief valve line and fresh dielectric fittings where metals meet.

  5. Check the vent or wiring

    On gas units we confirm draft and combustion. On electric units we check the circuit and the connections.

  6. Fill, purge and test

    We fill the tank, bleed the air, restore power or gas and confirm hot water at the fixtures.

Thermal expansion, permits and the city's guidance

When cold water heats inside a closed system, it expands and pressure rises. Brigham City publishes its own guidance on thermal expansion in water heating systems. An expansion tank is the usual answer, and if your old heater lacked one, a new heater is the time to add it.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting. If a permit applies, we explain what an inspection will look at. Skipping that step can complicate a home sale or an insurance claim later.

Hard water and how long the next tank lasts

The city's water page puts local hardness at 17 to 20 grains per gallon. Water that hard leaves mineral layers on the bottom of the tank, and the layers hold heat against the steel and speed up corrosion. That is a common reason a tank dies sooner than its label suggests.

You can protect the next one with a yearly flush and a fresh anode rod check, or take the cause away with a water softener. Ask Brigham City Public Works for the current hardness number before you decide on a softener size.

Swapping a gas heater for a heat pump

Some owners want to retire a gas tank and go electric. It is possible, but it is a bigger project than a like-for-like swap. The gas line has to be capped, the vent addressed, and a heat pump needs a dedicated circuit, open air space and a condensate drain. Plan the project on a scheduled visit, not at 2 a.m. with a flooded floor.
