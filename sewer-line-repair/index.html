Sewage coming back into the house: do this first

Sewage in a shower, a bathtub or the basement bath means the line out of the house cannot carry the flow. It is urgent, and our licensed plumbers answer 24/7. Stop using water, keep people and pets away from the wet area, and open a window if the smell is strong.

Sewer line repair is the work of fixing or replacing the buried pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public main or a septic tank. It follows a diagnosis. Cleaning the sewer line clears a stoppage, while repair fixes the pipe that caused it.

  1. Shut off the washer, dishwasher and every tap.
  2. Do not flush. Each flush adds to what is coming up.
  3. Do not use a plunger on the tub or floor drain. It pushes sewage out of the opposite opening.
  4. Wash hands and keep anything you touched away from food.

How a buried sewer pipe fails

Root intrusion

Fine roots enter at joints and swell until the pipe is partly filled with a mat of fibers.

Cracks and breaks

Soil movement, heavy loads or age can split a pipe along its length or snap it at a joint.

Sags, called bellies

A section settles, holds water and collects solids. Clogs return in the same place.

Offset joints

Two pipe ends shift sideways. Debris catches on the lip.

Collapse

The pipe flattens, and a cable stops at the same spot every time.

Symptoms callers describe before the line gives out

Most homeowners notice slow drains in the lowest fixtures first (a plain drain cleaning visit sometimes fixes that, but not when the cause is the pipe), then a gurgle in a toilet when the washer drains. Other signs include a sewer smell in a bathroom, a P-trap that will not hold water, and damp or unusually green patches over the line in the yard. A pre-purchase surprise is common too: a camera shows a belly or break nobody mentioned.

None of these proves the pipe is broken. A root-choked but intact pipe can be cleaned. That is why we confirm with video before proposing a dig or a liner.

Finding the break before anyone digs

  1. Cable and test

    A cable run shows how far it travels and where it stalls.

  2. Camera pass

    A push camera records the full run. We pause at the damaged spot and note the footage position.

  3. Locate and mark

    A locating receiver above ground picks up the camera head signal, so we can mark the spot and depth on your lawn or driveway.

  4. Review together

    You see the video and decide with full information.

Repair options and their trade-offs

Choosing a repair method.
MethodFitsTrade-off
Spot repairOne short broken sectionNeeds an excavation at that spot, but limits the dig.
Lining the pipeLong runs of cracked pipe or root-prone jointsLess digging, but it needs a clean pipe and does not fix a bad sag or collapse.
Pipe bursting or replacementCollapsed, offset or badly bellied pipeThe most work, with the longest-lasting result.
Lateral replacement to the mainFailed line from house to the public connectionNeeds coordination on who owns each segment.

Honest limits apply. Lining cannot bring back a collapsed pipe, and a spot repair does nothing for the next section if the whole run is old. We give you the options in plain terms, including the one that costs less now and may cost more later.

Dig or no dig

Trenchless methods keep the lawn, the driveway and the landscaping mostly intact. They are not always possible. A deep bellied section, a pipe with a collapse or a tight bend can force an open trench. Lining also depends on a clear path through the pipe, so cleaning, often with hydro jetting, usually comes first.

Replacing the lateral, the pipe between the house and the public connection, is the largest job we describe here. It makes sense when the camera shows damage along most of the run, since patching one spot leaves the weak pipe on both sides.

An open trench is more disruptive but lets us see the bedding, replace pipe on a correct grade and fix the real cause. The camera footage decides, not a sales preference.

Before the first shovel: 811 and who owns what

Blue Stakes of Utah asks you to call 811 before any excavation, at least 3 business days ahead and not more than 14 calendar days. Utility lines marked in the yard keep a repair from striking a gas or power line, so plan the dig date around it.

Ask Brigham City Public Works at (435) 734-6615 which segment belongs to the city and which to you. That decides who pays and who digs. Do not assume.

Septic is a different system

If your property uses a septic tank, a backup may come from the tank, the drain field or the pipe between. We can inspect and repair the plumbing between the house and the tank. Tank pumping is a different specialty, so ask a septic company about it.
