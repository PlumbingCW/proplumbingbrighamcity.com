Water on the floor of your business after closing

A flooded restroom, a kitchen drain that will not clear or a pipe spraying in the back room cannot wait until morning, and our commercial plumbing line in Brigham City, UT answers 24/7. Commercial plumbing is the repair and upkeep of the water, drain and waste systems in shops, restaurants, offices and rental buildings.

While you wait, find the building shutoff and close it if water is still running. Cut power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Move stock and paperwork off the floor, and tell dispatch if the water is clear, gray or dark. Same-day visits are offered when a crew is free.

After-hours drain stoppages in kitchens and restrooms

Commercial drains carry more load than home drains. A restaurant kitchen sends grease and food solids down the line all day, and a busy restroom sends paper and wipes. A single clogged fixture usually clears with a cable. Several fixtures backing up at once point to a shared line.

For ordinary clogs, our drain cleaning service uses cables sized to the pipe. When roots, scale or packed grease keep bringing the clog back, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall. If sewage is coming up through a floor drain, stop using water in the building until it is cleared.

Grease traps and interceptors

A grease trap sits between your kitchen fixtures and the building drain and catches fat before it hardens in the line. When it fills, the first signs are slow sinks, a sour smell, and drains that clear for a day and then back up again.

  • Slow dish sinks and prep sinks that all drain badly together.
  • A rotten or sour smell near the trap lid or floor drains.
  • Standing water in the trap that does not drop.
  • Repeated clogs in the line just past the trap.

We clear the lines on both sides of the trap and check that baffles and fittings are intact. The pump-out of the trap itself is often handled by a separate hauler, so ask on the call who does what. A trap on a regular schedule usually costs less than repeated emergency calls.

Floor drains that overflow or smell

A floor drain in a storeroom, kitchen or mechanical room is a safety valve. When it backs up, the building line is usually blocked downstream. Pour a bucket of water into the drain. If it rises or gurgles, the stoppage is in the line and not the grate.

A dry drain also causes smell, because the trap seal evaporates and lets sewer gas in. Adding water cures that. If water stays down and the odor stays, we run a camera to see whether a cracked line or a bad vent is the cause. A grate stuck over an animal, debris or a lost item is also a call we take.

Commercial leak detection: what it can and cannot do

Leak detection on a business building is honest, slow work, and not every visit ends in a pinpoint. We use listening equipment on exposed lines, pressure tests on isolated sections, and a camera in drain lines. A leak under a slab, under a large parking area or inside a long buried main may take more than one visit.

If your water meter keeps moving after you shut every fixture, that is the first clue. Residents may not open the city meter, so call Brigham City Public Works at (435) 734-6615. Our leak detection page explains the steps in more detail. A resort-wide or fire main survey needs specialized equipment, so ask on the phone before you book.

Storm drains and yard drains around the building

Roof leaders, parking area drains and trench drains fill with leaves, grit and roots. A quick cable run clears a plugged catch basin. A line that clogs every spring thaw usually needs jetting and a camera look at the joints.

Brigham City averages about 38 inches of snow a year, and melt arrives all at once. Clear grates and basins before the first big thaw, because backed-up yard drains send water toward doors and loading areas.

Backflow and cross-connection: planned work

A cross-connection is any link that could let used or contaminated water flow back into the building supply. A backflow assembly is the device that stops it. Brigham City publishes cross-connection control and backflow guidance, and Public Works is the office to ask about what applies to your property.

This is scheduled work, not an emergency call. The plumbing side includes where an assembly sits, access for service, and replacement or related piping. Tell us on the phone what you need so we confirm the scope and who handles testing before anyone is sent. New tenant build-outs and relocations can fold into plumbing installation plans.
