Active failure

The pump quit and water is rising right now

A failed sump pump is an urgent call, and our dispatch line runs 24/7 for exactly this. A sump pump is a pump in a pit at the lowest point of your floor that moves groundwater out and away from the house, and when it stops, the pit fills and then the floor does.

Callers describe a pit near the brim, a pump that hums without moving water, or a basement that was dry an hour ago. Start the steps below while you wait.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  1. Stay out of standing water

    If water is near outlets, a panel or an appliance, keep out and have an electrician or the power utility cut power safely.

  2. Check the plug and breaker

    A tripped GFCI outlet or breaker is the simplest cause. Reset once. If it trips again, leave it off.

  3. Lift the float gently

    With the pump unplugged, see whether the float moves freely. A stuck float means the pump never gets told to run.

  4. Bail or siphon if you can

    A wet-dry vacuum or buckets to a floor drain, utility sink or outdoors buys time. Do not pour water into the pit.

  5. Move belongings up

    Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor, then call so we can plan same-day or after-hours service.

Most pumps fail because they get used hard in a few wet weeks, then sit idle for months. The first big storm of the season is often when a worn float or tired motor finally gives out.

Testing the pit before the wet season

Test a pump twice a year, and again before spring thaw. Pour a few gallons of water slowly into the pit. The float should rise, the pump should start, and the pit should drain within seconds.

Watch the outside end of the discharge pipe while it runs. If water spills right beside the foundation or the pipe is frozen shut, the water can loop back to the pit. A pump that runs constantly with no rain may point to a leaking supply pipe, which leak detection can sort out.

Repair or replace: how to decide

A float switch, check valve or clogged screen is a quick repair. A worn motor, a cracked housing or a pump that has run through many seasons usually costs more to chase than to replace.

Replacement also gives a chance to fix what caused the failure: a pit that is too shallow, a pump that is undersized for the water it handles, or a discharge that dumps too close to the house. Planned installs for a new pit or an upgrade are part of our plumbing installation work.

Battery backup and where the water goes

Storms that overload a pump also tend to knock out power. A battery backup pump runs from a sealed battery and a second float, so the pit still empties during an outage. It does not last forever, and it needs a charged battery and a test.

The discharge line matters just as much. It should carry water well away from the foundation and should not tie into the sewer. Ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 about local rules, since permit rules vary.

Sewage ejector pumps and basement bathrooms

A sewage ejector is a different machine. It lifts waste from a basement bath or laundry up to the main drain, so a failure means sewer smell, gurgling or wastewater coming up in a floor drain. Do not use the fixtures downstairs until it is fixed.

If wastewater is backing up and the pump is not the cause, a clogged line may be the culprit. See drain cleaning for that side.

When it is not a pump problem

  • Standing water near electrical equipment: power utility or an electrician comes first.
  • Cracks letting groundwater through a wall: a foundation or waterproofing contractor, see basement waterproofing.
  • City-main sewer backups: call Public Works at (435) 734-6615, or the after-hours water emergency line at (435) 723-6868.
  • Wet floors from a burst supply line: shut off the main and ask for plumbing help.
