A plumber's share of keeping a basement dry

Basement waterproofing is the set of steps that keep water out of a below-grade space or carry it away fast once it gets in. Part of that work is plumbing: drains, pumps, discharge lines and finding leaking pipes.

We are plumbers. Cracked foundation walls, failed membranes and excavation around footings belong to foundation and waterproofing contractors, and we will say so plainly instead of selling you something outside our trade.

Diagnosis first

Groundwater, condensation or a pipe leak?

Wet basements get blamed on the walls, but a surprising number trace back to a pipe. A basement that dampens only after rain or thaw leans toward groundwater. One that stays wet in dry weeks leans toward plumbing.

A simple home test to separate pipes from groundwater

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Look at the meter or ask Public Works about a reading. Residents may not open city meters, so call (435) 734-6615 for help, and note that the city offers water pressure testing.

If the dial or leak indicator keeps moving with everything off, water is escaping somewhere. Our leak detection process uses listening gear, pressure checks and moisture readings to find it.

If the meter holds still and the floor only sweats after storms, the water is coming from outside the pipes.

The pit, the pump and the interior drain

When groundwater gets in, the usual plumbing answer is to give it a channel and a place to go. That means a perimeter drain channel along the inside of the footing, leading to a pit with a pump that sends water outside.

  • Pit and pump: sized for the water the basement actually sees, with a check valve and discharge line.
  • Floor drains: kept clear so they carry water out, not back up.
  • Backup power: a second pump or battery keeps the pit emptying in a storm outage.
  • Discharge route: ends well away from the foundation and not in the sewer.

A floor drain that backs up needs drain cleaning before anyone blames the walls. Cutting the slab for an interior channel is heavy work. Ask us whether we handle your layout, and expect an honest answer if a specialist fits better. Repairs to a failing pump are covered on our sump pump repair page.

Window wells, downspouts and the lot outside

A good share of basement water starts outside. Gutters that spill beside the wall, soil that slopes toward the house, and window wells with no drainage all put water against the foundation.

Extending downspouts, clearing window wells and adding a drain at the bottom of a deep well are modest fixes. Regrading a yard is landscaping or excavation work. If a yard dig is planned, call 811 (or 800-662-4111) at least 3 business days ahead and no more than 14 calendar days before you start.

Where plumbing ends and another trade begins

A plumber can move water, find pipe leaks, clear drains and install pumps. A plumber cannot judge whether a wall is structurally sound, repair a cracked footing or apply exterior membranes.

If a wall is bowing, a crack widens, or stair-step cracks run through block, bring in a foundation contractor or structural engineer. Fixing pipes and pumps first often shows which kind of help you actually need.

What the 2018 Box Elder Creek overflow says about runoff

When Box Elder Creek overflowed on March 23, 2018, debris and melting snow pushed water out of the channel, about 10 homes had flooding, including basements, and thousands of sandbags went up. That kind of flood is largely surface water, which plumbing cannot stop.

That matters for planning. A pump handles water that reaches the pit, but it will not hold back a creek. If you live near a drainage path, think about where runoff travels around your lot and ask what a drain and pump can realistically do. Homeowners in Willard, about 7 miles south, can ask the same questions about their own lots.

A sensible order for dealing with a wet basement

  1. Track when it gets wet

    Note whether water follows rain, thaw or nothing at all.

  2. Rule out pipes

    Check the meter with fixtures off and look under bathrooms and laundry.

  3. Fix the outside basics

    Downspouts, window wells and grade come before anything buried.

  4. Add or repair drainage

    Install or service the pit, pump, floor drain and discharge.

  5. Bring in other trades if needed

    Cracks and structural movement need a foundation contractor.

If drain work or new pipe runs are part of the plan, our plumbing installation crews can handle the plumbing portion.
