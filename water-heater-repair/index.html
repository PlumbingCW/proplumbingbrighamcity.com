Cold showers or a wet floor? Do this before we arrive

Water heater repair means diagnosing why a tank or its controls have stopped making hot water, then fixing the failed part if the tank is still sound. Dispatch is open 24/7, and same-day visits are available when the schedule allows. While you wait, shut the cold supply valve above the heater, cut the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot or off, and put a bucket under any drip.

Do not drain a hot tank by hand until it has cooled, because the water can scald. Take a photo of the data label on the side of the tank. Model, size and fuel type let the technician plan parts before the truck leaves.

Water pooling around the heater: leak or condensation?

A heater leak is the call we hear most at odd hours: a pan filling, a damp garage floor, a stain on the ceiling below the closet. The first job is to learn where the water actually comes from, because the answer decides everything else.

  • Drips at the top: usually an inlet or outlet fitting, a nipple or a dielectric union. These are fixable.
  • Water from the temperature and pressure relief valve: often a pressure or thermal expansion problem, or a valve that has worn out.
  • Water at the drain valve near the bottom: a loose or damaged valve, which is a modest repair.
  • Water seeping from the tank body or the bottom seam: the tank has rusted through. No patch lasts, so the honest answer is replacement.

Light moisture on a cold tank in summer can also be condensation, not a leak. Wipe it dry and look again in an hour. If the tank is the source, our water heater replacement crew can usually set a new one the same day when parts and schedule allow.

No hot water at all: the urgent checklist

Nobody can shower, and the dishwasher is running cold. A heater that has stopped heating is almost always one of a short list of failures, and a technician can narrow it down in minutes. Some of the checks are safe for you to try first, and some are not.

Gas heaters and the smell of gas

A gas water heater has a burner, a flue and a gas control, and each can fail without a leak. A rotten-egg smell is different. If you smell gas, leave the house first. Once you are safely away, call 911 or the gas utility's 24-hour emergency line, 1-800-767-1689. Do not use a light switch, a phone inside the house or a lighter.

  • A yellow, lazy flame or soot around the draft hood means incomplete combustion and needs a service call.
  • Water dripping into the burner chamber can put the pilot out again and again.
  • A pilot that lights but will not stay lit usually points to a worn thermocouple.
  • Sooty stains, a hot flue pipe at the wall or any dizziness means leave and call for help.

What 17 to 20 grains of hardness does inside a tank

Brigham City's own water page lists hardness at 17 to 20 grains per gallon, which counts as very hard on the USGS scale. In a heater, that mineral load bakes into a crust on gas tank floors and on electric elements. A crust acts like a blanket, so burners run longer, elements burn out sooner and the tank rumbles when the water boils underneath.

Flushing the tank once a year clears loose sediment and extends the life of the parts we repair. A water softener slows the build-up for good, and ask Public Works for the current hardness figure before you size one. If you are thinking about changing the whole setup, tankless water heater options need scale control even more.

Parts we repair, and parts that tell us to stop

Heating elements and thermostats

Common electric failures. They are inexpensive to swap compared with a new tank, if the tank is healthy.

Thermocouple, pilot assembly, gas control

The usual gas culprits. A new gas control is not worth buying for a tank that is also leaking.

Anode rod

The sacrificial rod that protects the tank wall. Replacing a spent one can add years, but only if the tank is not already corroded.

Relief valve and fittings

Cheap parts that cause messy leaks. We replace them and check the expansion setup that made the valve open.

Drain valve and flush

A seized valve is swapped and the tank is flushed to move out the sediment.

There is a point where repair stops making sense. A heater with a rusted tank, a failed repair this year or a recurring leak is a replacement candidate. We tell you which side of that line your unit is on before we start.

Heat pump and hybrid units

Hybrid and heat pump water heaters add a compressor and a fan on top of a normal electric tank. They still use elements as backup, so a cold shower does not always mean the heat pump has failed. Condensate drain clogs, blocked air filters and tripped breakers are common reasons for errors, and we check those first. Compressor repair is a specialist item, and we will say so if that is what the unit needs.

When it is not a plumbing job

  • A gas smell is a utility and 911 matter first, as covered above.
  • A breaker that trips again as soon as you reset it points to wiring, so call an electrician.
  • No water at any tap, hot or cold, may be a city supply problem. Brigham City's after-hours water line is (435) 723-6868.
  • Flood damage to drywall, carpet and insulation needs a drying contractor after the leak is stopped.
  • A leaking tank that has been repaired twice is cheaper to replace than to keep chasing.
