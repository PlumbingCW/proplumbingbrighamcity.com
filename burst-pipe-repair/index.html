Water gushing from a pipe: the first five minutes

A split pipe can flood a room fast, so speed matters. Our licensed plumbers answer 24/7. Shut the water off first, then call, and keep the line open so dispatch can walk you through what is left.

A burst pipe is a supply or drain line that has cracked, split or separated enough to release water in volume, usually from freezing, age, pressure or a failed joint. Burst pipe repair removes the damaged section and restores a sound connection.

  1. Turn off the main shutoff valve inside the house.
  2. Open the lowest cold tap to drain the pressure.
  3. Switch off the water heater if the broken line feeds it.
  4. Move electronics, rugs and boxes out of the wet area.
  5. Photograph the damage for your insurance file.

Why pipes split in a Brigham City January

Brigham City's average January low is 18.1 F, and the city sees about 38.3 inches of snow a year. Water freezes at 32 F, and ice expands as it forms, which squeezes the pipe wall from inside. The split is often in an unheated crawlspace, garage or exterior wall, and it shows up as a flood when the ice melts.

That timing is the trap. The pipe breaks while frozen, then leaks once the temperature rises. If a tap trickles in a cold snap, shut the main and call before the thaw. Heat tape, insulation and letting a tap drip help, but they are prevention, not repair.

Shutting off water: house valve first, street side second

  1. Find the main shutoff

    It is usually where the water line enters the house, in a basement, utility room or garage.

  2. Turn it clockwise

    Turn it fully. If it will not move, or it is broken, say so on the call.

  3. Try the fixture valves

    A supply valve under a sink or behind a toilet isolates one fixture.

  4. Call the city for the street side

    Residents are not permitted to access water meters. Call Public Works at (435) 734-6615, or the after-hours water line at (435) 723-6868.

A stuck or broken main valve is a plumbing problem too. We replace it so that the next emergency has a working shutoff.

Repair options and honest trade-offs

  • Cut and replace gives the strongest result and works best when the damage is short and the pipe around it is sound.
  • A sleeve or coupling is quick and good for a single pinhole, though it does not help a pipe that is failing in several places.
  • A temporary clamp stops the water tonight but is a stopgap, not a permanent answer.
  • Multiple bursts in one season usually mean the whole run is tired, and a longer-term fix deserves discussion, including repiping.

We explain which one we recommend and why before work starts. We will not push a bigger job than the pipe needs.

When the broken valve leaves you with no water

A broken or stuck valve is its own emergency. Callers describe a handle that spins loose, a valve that will not shut or a line that sprays with no way to stop it. Without a working shutoff, the house stays wet until the street side is closed.

Call Public Works for the street side, then call us. Once the water is off, we replace the valve and restore service. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and urgent calls go out at night and on weekends.

Finding the water you cannot see

Some bursts hide inside a wall or under a floor. Signs are a stained ceiling, a wet baseboard, a hissing sound, a meter that keeps spinning with everything off, or a sudden drop in pressure. We use a moisture meter, listening gear and a pressure test to trace the line. If the source stays unclear, leak detection is the right next step.

If the break is on the hot side, check the water heater too. A leak at the tank connection can look like a burst pipe, and our water heater repair page covers that case.

When it is not a plumber job

If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, leave the power off and call an electrician. If a gas smell is present, get out first, then call 911 or the gas utility. If walls and floors are soaked, a water restoration company handles drying. We stop the water and fix the pipe, and these other trades finish what we start.
