What downtown means on this page

Downtown here means Main Street and the nearby blocks, not a formal boundary. The corridor holds the Box Elder Stake Tabernacle, which is on the National Register, the Brigham City Museum-Gallery, Hotel Brigham, and the Brigham City Utah Temple, dedicated September 23, 2012. The city's building office is also on Main Street, at 20 N. Main on the second floor.

Downtown sits inside the Brigham City Historic District. The district nomination counts 79 contributing commercial buildings, but commercial buildings are only about 7 percent of what contributes. Most of the district is homes, and those blocks are covered on the historic district page.

Plumbing for shops, restaurants and offices

These are general commercial plumbing points, not a description of any specific building. A kitchen drain carries grease and food solids, so a food business watches for slow sinks and floor drains before they back up. A restroom with several fixtures can lose them all at once when the shared line clogs. A business that depends on hot water, such as a kitchen or a salon, notices a failing water heater the same day.

Our commercial plumbing page covers service for business properties. When a line keeps clogging, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the blockage, and drain cleaning covers the common cases.

Shared walls, upper floors and old basements

Downtown blocks often mix a business at street level with offices or apartments above. A leak from above can come from a unit you do not control, so find out who owns the shutoff before water is running. If you rent, tell the landlord at the same time you call.

A slow stain that returns after you dry it points to a hidden leak. Use leak detection to locate it before opening a wall, and ask about water heater repair when hot water runs out early.

Peach Days and Main Street closures

Peach Days is held the weekend after Labor Day, and much of Main Street is closed to cars. That changes how anyone reaches a building. When you call during that weekend, give the cross street and say whether there is alley or rear access.

A kitchen with a slow drain has less room to wait when the weekend is busy, so test drains and water heaters in the week before.

Backflow, permits and digging

The city publishes pages on backflow prevention, cross-connection control and thermal expansion in water heating systems. Which of them apply to your building is a question for Brigham City Public Works at (435) 734-6615. The meter belongs to the city, so residents and tenants should not touch it.

Permit rules vary, so ask the building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting work. If a job means digging in a yard, alley or the street, call 811 at least three business days beforehand, so buried lines get marked.

What to tell the plumber

Say whether the building is a business, a home or both, which floor the problem is on, and what the nearest cross street is. Say whether the tenant and the owner are different people, and who can authorize work. Then ask what service is available and what the next step would be.
