Water backing up in your house right now

A drain that spits water back up is an emergency in the making, and our licensed plumbers answer the phone 24/7 for it. While you wait, stop sending water down that line, pull rugs and cardboard off the floor, and keep the chemical drain opener on the shelf, because mixed chemicals give off dangerous fumes.

Drain cleaning in Brigham City means removing the blockage from a fixture drain or branch line so water flows again, using a hand auger, a power cable or a camera to see what is stuck. Grease, hair, soap film and wipes cause most of it.

  • Turn off the washer and dishwasher if they share the clogged line.
  • Do not plunge a toilet and a tub on the same line, because pressure pushes water out the weakest opening.
  • Move anything valuable away from floor drains and baseboards.
  • Tell us which fixtures are slow, which gurgle and which are fully stopped. That pattern shows us where to start.

How a clogged drain announces itself

Callers describe the same handful of things. The kitchen sink drains slowly, then not at all. The tub fills while the shower runs. The washer drain gurgles or spits at the standpipe. A toilet flushes weakly and the shower drain bubbles at the same moment.

Two signs deserve extra attention. A sewer-gas smell in a bathroom, or water showing up at the baseboards or a basement floor drain, usually means the blockage is deeper than a single fixture. If several drains act up together, the stoppage sits on a shared line, and a quick fixture-level fix will not hold.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food scraps and a shared line

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and coats the wall, and each rinse leaves a thin new layer. Over months the opening shrinks to a slot, and a lump of rice or coffee grounds finishes the job.

A sink that backs up into the laundry sink or dishwasher tells us the blockage is past the trap, not in it. We open the trap first to rule out a simple jam, then run a cable down the wall line. If a disposal sits in the line, we check that it is not the culprit. Our disposal repair page covers the jams and leaks that mimic a clog.

A wet cabinet floor is a separate issue. A loose slip nut or cracked trap causes it, and we check joints before cabling because a cable can loosen a weak fitting.

What snaking and cabling actually do inside the pipe

  1. Find the access point

    We use the trap, a cleanout or a removed fixture. The closest opening to the clog saves the most cable.

  2. Feed the cable

    A spinning head runs down the line until it meets resistance. The feel of the stop tells us if it is soft (grease), stringy (hair) or hard (a foreign object).

  3. Break or hook the blockage

    The head cuts a hole through the clog or pulls it back out. Pulling out beats pushing through, since pushed debris settles again downstream.

  4. Flush and test

    We run hot water and then a heavy flow to see whether the drain keeps up under real use.

  5. Camera if needed

    If the clog returns, a camera shows whether the pipe is scarred, sagging or invaded by roots.

A cable has limits. A short cable fails on a long run, and a thin cable pokes a hole in a grease wall that can close again. A cable also cannot clean the pipe wall the way water can, which is why repeat clogs often call for hydro jetting after the first clearing.

Tub, shower and washer drains

Tub and shower drains clog with hair and soap scum, usually in the first few inches below the strainer or in the trap. If the water stands after you pull the stopper, we remove the strainer, pull what we can reach and cable the rest.

Washer drains clog on lint and detergent residue. The standpipe fills, then overflows onto the laundry floor in the middle of a cycle. A cable from the standpipe usually clears it, and a lint filter on the washer hose helps keep it clear.

A drain line leak behind a wall or under a floor looks like a clog at first: slow water, damp drywall, a musty odor. If the line is cracked, cleaning it only moves water to the leak faster. That is when a camera earns its keep, and when a repair, not a cleaning, is the right answer.

When it is bigger than a drain

Some stoppages are not a fixture problem. If sewage comes up in the lowest tub or floor drain whenever the washer runs, the main sewer line is likely blocked, and our sewer line cleaning page explains how that job differs. If the clog keeps returning and a cable finds nothing, a camera inspection shows what the pipe is hiding.

Some calls are not ours at all. If you smell gas, leave the building first, then call 911 or the gas utility. If water has reached outlets or appliances, shut off power to that area at the panel only if the panel is dry, and call an electrician. A condensate line from an air conditioner belongs to an HVAC tech.
