When a plumbing problem will not wait until morning

An emergency plumber is a licensed plumber who can be sent to your home or business right away, at any hour, when water, sewage or a failed appliance is putting the building at risk. Brigham City Pro Plumbing runs that kind of dispatch for Brigham City, UT, 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

A pipe does not pick a convenient time to split. Calls come in at 2 a.m. when a water heater tank gives out, on Saturday afternoons when a kitchen drain backs up, and on holiday mornings when a supply line lets go behind a wall. The line is staffed for exactly those moments.

Same-day appointments are offered when available. We do not promise an arrival time, because honest timing depends on where crews are and how many urgent calls are ahead of yours. The dispatcher will tell you what is realistic for your address.

First ten minutes

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  1. Stop the water

    Turn the main shutoff valve clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, close the fixture valve nearest the leak and tell dispatch.

  2. Cut power near the water

    If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, switch off that circuit at the breaker only if the floor around the panel is dry. Otherwise stay back and call the electric utility.

  3. Move people and belongings

    Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off wet floors. Keep children and pets out of any room with sewage.

  4. Catch and contain

    Put a bucket under drips, towels along the edge of a spreading puddle, and open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line after shutoff.

  5. Take photos

    Photograph the damage before you mop. Your homeowners insurer may ask for them later.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or look for the source. Leave the building first, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

What to tell dispatch so the right plumber is sent

The more precise your description, the better the first visit goes. A dispatcher is listening for a few specific details, and having them ready saves minutes.

  • The street address and the nearest cross street, so the crew can find you quickly.
  • What you see: dripping, spraying, sewage, a stained ceiling or a meter that keeps spinning.
  • Whether the water is shut off and where you found the valve.
  • Which fixture or appliance is involved, and whether it is gas or electric if it is a water heater.
  • Whether anyone in the building has limited mobility, or if a business needs to stay open.
  • The best phone number to reach you if the call drops.

You do not need to know the cause. Describing the symptom is enough, and the plumber will diagnose the rest on site.

Brigham City conditions that drive after-hours calls

Winters here are cold enough to matter. The average January low is 18.1 F and the city sees about 38.3 inches of snow a year, so exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces and outside walls are the usual suspects when a line splits. If you leave for a trip, keep the heat on and know where your main valve is.

The water itself is hard. The Brigham City Water Division lists 17 to 20 grains per gallon on its water page, which is very hard on the USGS scale. Over time that scale builds on heating elements and in tank bottoms, shortens water heater life and clogs aerators. A water softener is one way to deal with it, and Public Works can confirm the current figure.

Spring runoff is the other risk. On March 23, 2018, Box Elder Creek overflowed and about 10 homes had flooding, some in basements. If your basement takes on water during snowmelt, a working sump pump is worth testing before the season starts.

If the problem is on the street side of your meter, residents are not permitted to open city meters. Call Brigham City Public Works at (435) 734-6615 or the after-hours water line at (435) 723-6868, then call us for anything inside the property line.

Downtown, Peach Days and finding your address fast

Main Street runs through the older part of downtown, so tell dispatch your cross street. Older blocks near downtown may still have galvanized or older copper lines. A camera or inspection confirms what yours actually has, so do not assume.

Peach Days falls on the weekend after Labor Day, and much of Main Street is closed to cars. If you call during that weekend, tell dispatch which side street to use and where to park, because a van cannot cut through the closed blocks. The same advice applies to any business on Main Street that needs a plumber after hours.

Directions help year-round. Mention if you are near US-89 or US-91 on the way into Box Elder Canyon, or close to the I-15 interchange. Those landmarks let a dispatcher place you in seconds.

Checking that any plumber is licensed

Utah licenses plumbers by class: apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master and residential master. You can check any plumber, including ours, on the Utah Division of Professional Licensing Verify a License tool at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html. It takes a minute and requires only a name.

Brigham City Pro Plumbing dispatches licensed and insured plumbers. Ask for the plumber's name when the crew confirms the visit, then look it up if you want certainty. A reputable plumber will welcome the check.
