Planning a water heater install the right way

Water heater installation is the planned work of choosing a unit, preparing the space and connecting it to water, fuel and a safe vent. It is different from an emergency swap, because you have time to compare options and fix the problems the old setup carried. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, but most installs are booked ahead.

Good installs start with questions: who lives in the house, how many baths run at once, what fuel is available, and where the unit will sit. The answers shape everything that follows.

Sizing the unit to how your household really lives

Sizing is where many installs go wrong. A unit that is too small leaves the last person in line with a cold shower, and one that is too large wastes space and fuel. The right choice follows your peak demand, not your square footage.

Matching a household pattern to a sizing question.
Household patternWhat to askUsual direction
Two people, one bathDo showers ever overlap with the dishwasher?A modest tank usually fits.
Family with morning rushHow many hot taps run in the same half hour?A larger tank or high-recovery unit.
Large soaking tub or multiple bathsCan the supply refill fast enough?Larger tank or tankless sized to flow.
Frequent guests or a rental unitDoes demand swing between empty and full?Tankless or a bigger tank.
Workshop or laundry heavy homeDoes laundry run alongside showers?Extra recovery speed.

If you are leaning toward on-demand heat, the tankless water heater page explains flow, gas supply and scale. For a broken tank that needs immediate help, emergency water heater replacement is the faster path.

Where it goes: location, clearances and venting

The spot matters as much as the unit. A heater needs a level base, a drain pan with a drain line that runs to a safe place, and room around it for service. Gas models need combustion air and a vent pipe sized and pitched correctly. A bad vent can spill exhaust into the house.

Moving the unit is a common reason for a planned install. Owners shift heaters out of cramped closets, away from bedrooms, or closer to the kitchen and baths to shorten the wait for hot water. Longer runs mean more piping, so we map the route before quoting.

Before we arrive: preparing the space

  • Clear a path from the door to the heater and leave a work area around it.
  • Find the main water shutoff and the gas shutoff and show them to the plumber.
  • Move stored items away from the heater, especially anything flammable.
  • Ask about permits, because rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting.
  • Keep children and pets out of the work area.

Gas, electric or heat pump: fuel decides the install

  • Gas: needs a gas line sized for the load, a shutoff, a sediment trap and a vent that terminates safely.
  • Electric: needs a circuit sized to the unit and a breaker that matches. The wire gauge is not a guess.
  • Heat pump: needs open air space, a condensate drain and a dedicated circuit.
  • Switching fuels: plan the work in advance, because capping a gas line or adding a circuit takes more than one trade day.

If you ever smell gas near a heater, leave first, then call 911 or the gas utility. Gas work belongs to licensed hands, and our plumbing installation crews handle the supply side.

The expansion tank and pressure side

Heating water in a closed system raises pressure. Brigham City publishes guidance on thermal expansion in water heating systems, and an expansion tank is the common fix. It gets sized and charged to your house pressure and mounted on the cold line. Ask Public Works about pressure testing if you suspect high street pressure.

New construction and remodel timing

In a new build or a gut remodel, the heater is usually set after rough-in and before final inspection. Tell us the plan early. We can place stub-outs, vent paths and drain locations while the walls are open, which avoids cutting later.

Because local water is hard, a softener can share the same utility space, and a bypass loop makes future service easier. That is a single conversation worth having during the rough-in stage.

Permits, licensing and who should do the work

Permit rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting. They can tell you whether your install needs a permit and which code edition applies. Utah adopts plumbing codes through the state.

You can also confirm that any plumber is licensed. The Utah Division of Professional Licensing offers a Verify a License tool, and it lists classes from apprentice to master. Check the name against the person on your doorstep, ours or anyone else's.

Install day, start to finish

  1. Walk-through

    We confirm the location, fuel, venting path and any code items on site.

  2. Shut off and set up

    Water is closed, the area is protected and the old unit is drained if one exists.

  3. Set the new heater

    The unit goes on its pan or base, level and clear of combustibles.

  4. Connect and vent

    Water, fuel, relief valve, expansion tank and vent are connected and checked.

  5. Fill, fire and test

    We purge air, restore power or gas, check for leaks and confirm temperature at the fixtures.

  6. Walk you through it

    You see where the shutoffs are and how to set the temperature.
