Main line clogged: sewage in the tub, toilets that bubble

When the main line stops, water has nowhere to go but out of the lowest opening, often a basement shower, a tub or a floor drain. We answer 24/7 for that. While you wait, stop all water use, keep the washer off and move belongings away from the lowest drain.

Sewer line cleaning is the removal of a blockage from the large pipe that carries all of your home's wastewater out to the street connection. It is a different job from clearing one sink, since a single clog there affects one fixture and this one affects every fixture at once.

We aim for same-day appointments when the schedule allows, and the line is open after hours for backups.

Finding the cleanout, and why you should not open it blind

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the sewer line. It is often outside near the foundation, in a basement wall, a utility room or a garage. Look for a round or square cap about 3 or 4 inches across.

If sewage is already backing up, do not unscrew the cap. A full line can push sewage out of an open cleanout. We open it carefully, with a plan for any overflow. If you cannot find one, say so on the call. Without a cleanout, we may need to pull a toilet or work through a vent.

  • Look along the foundation near where the water line enters.
  • Check the basement floor near the main drain and the laundry area.
  • Snap a photo of the cap if you find it, or describe where it sits when you call.

What to do while you wait for the crew

  • Stop using every drain in the house, including the washer and the dishwasher.
  • Do not run water to test whether the line is clear, because it adds to the backup.
  • Keep children and pets out of any room with sewage on the floor.
  • Wear gloves and boots if you must move belongings, then wash your hands well.
  • Photograph the backup before cleanup for your insurance claim.

Early warnings before the line stops

A main line rarely fails without hints. Callers describe a toilet that gurgles when the tub drains, a sewer smell near the basement floor drain, or water that lingers in a shower for a minute after the laundry finishes. Each one means air and water are fighting for room in a narrowing pipe.

Roots, grease, wipes and heavy paper are the usual cause. A line that has been clearing slowly for weeks is cheaper to deal with than one that has backed up into a finished basement. If the signs show up on a Friday evening, call that night instead of waiting for Monday.

How the main line gets cleared

  1. Cable with a cutter head

    A heavy-duty cable cuts through the blockage and restores flow quickly.

  2. Retrieve what is loose

    We pull out wipes, grease balls and root clumps, not push them downstream.

  3. Jet if the wall is coated

    When grease or scale remains, hydro jetting scours the pipe, so the stoppage does not return in weeks.

  4. Camera check

    A camera shows whether the pipe is intact or needs sewer line repair. A stand-alone sewer camera inspection is also available.

Tree roots are the commonest cause of repeat main line stoppages. Cutting them gets water moving, but roots will return if the joint stays open, so we tell you what the camera shows. Wipes labeled flushable are a frequent culprit too, since they do not break up like toilet paper.

City main or your own lateral: who to call in Brigham City

The public sewer main belongs to the city. The pipe from your house to that connection is your responsibility. Ask Brigham City Public Works at (435) 734-6615 who owns which part, and tell them if neighbors have the same backup, since that points to the main.
