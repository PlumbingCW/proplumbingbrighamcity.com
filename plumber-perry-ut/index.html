The main valve will not turn

Older gate valves seize from mineral build-up, and a stuck handle turns a small leak into a flooded room. Do not lean on it with a pipe wrench, because a cracked valve body turns a drip into a fountain.

A drain that backs up after dark

A tub that fills with gray water when the washer drains, or a toilet that gurgles when you run the kitchen sink, usually points to a blockage past the fixture. If several drains slow at once, the clog is in the main line, and the next flush can push sewage into the lowest drain in the house. Stop running water and keep everyone off the toilets until the line is cleared.

A night or weekend backup should not wait for morning. We clear lines at any hour, and the details are on our drain cleaning page.

Water in the basement or a sump pit that will not stop

Perry has a flood record. Water came out of Three Mile Creek Canyon in 1896 and again in 1923, and the town's first culinary water system was built in 1911 from mountain springs. That history says the hillside above town can send water down fast, so a basement here deserves a working pump.

We cannot tell you which streets ever took water, so judge by your own basement. Pour a bucket into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump discharge. If it hums without moving water, or the pit is already full, call for sump pump repair and a battery backup, because storms often knock out power at the same moment.

No hot water, or a tank weeping at the base

A tank that drips from the bottom seam has failed inside, and repair rarely fixes it. Shut the cold supply to the heater and, on a gas unit, turn the control to pilot. If you smell gas, leave the house first and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.
