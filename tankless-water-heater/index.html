How a tankless heater works, and who it suits

A tankless water heater heats water on demand as it passes through a heat exchanger, so there is no tank to run dry. It suits households that run back-to-back showers and want to free floor space. The trade-offs are a larger gas or electric load, careful venting and regular maintenance.

Most of the calls we hear fall into three groups: owners who need a flush, owners with an error code, and owners deciding whether to switch from a tank. This page follows that order.

Flushing and descaling the heat exchanger

Brigham City's own water page lists hardness at 17 to 20 grains per gallon. In a tankless unit, that mineral load settles inside narrow passages of the heat exchanger and slowly chokes flow, which shows up as lukewarm water, noisy operation and error codes. A scaled exchanger can shorten the life of an otherwise good unit.

  1. Isolate the unit

    Close the gas and cold water, and shut the hot outlet so the unit can be drained safely.

  2. Connect the flush kit

    A small pump and a bucket circulate a descaling solution through the exchanger.

  3. Circulate

    The solution runs for a set time, depending on how scaled the unit is.

  4. Rinse and clean the filter

    Fresh water flushes out loosened scale, and the inlet screen gets cleaned.

  5. Restore and test

    We reopen the valves, fire the unit and watch temperature and flow.

A yearly flush beats waiting for a warning code. A water softener ahead of the unit cuts the build-up, though ask Public Works for the current hardness before sizing it.

Error codes and what they usually point to

Codes differ by maker, so read your manual for the exact meaning. Callers often mention two-digit codes in the low teens, and the cause usually falls into a few families.

Sizing: flow rate and gas supply

Tankless sizing is about flow, not tank gallons. Add up the fixtures that may run at once, then match the temperature rise your incoming water needs. Cold winter inlet water means the unit works harder, so ask the installer to size for your coldest months.

  • Check the gas meter and the gas line size, because many tankless units demand more fuel than a tank.
  • A line that is too small starves the burner and triggers shutoffs.
  • Count showers, the dishwasher and the washer, not square footage.
  • Confirm the vent route and clearance before you pick a model.

Our water heater installation page covers venting and layout, and plumbing installation work handles the gas and water lines.

Switching from a tank to tankless

A conversion is bigger than a like-for-like swap. Expect a new vent, possibly a bigger gas line, a condensate drain on condensing models, and an outlet for the unit's electronics. A pressure relief and a service valve kit make future flushing easy.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting. If you smell gas at any point, leave first, then call 911 or the gas utility.

Repair or replace: the service-not-just-replace worry

Some installers only want to replace. We start by finding the actual fault, whether that is a sensor, a flow switch, a control board or scale. If the exchanger is cracked or a repair would cost close to a new unit, we say so. If a flush or a part brings the unit back, that is what we do first.
