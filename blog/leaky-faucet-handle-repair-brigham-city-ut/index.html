Leaky Faucet Handle Repair in Brigham City, UT: Find the Type, Then the Part

Leaky faucet handle repair in Brigham City, UT starts with naming your handle type, because each one hides a different worn part.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

Start here: water at the handle means a worn seal, not a dead faucet

Water that creeps out around the base of a handle almost always comes from a worn O-ring, a loose packing nut, or a tired cartridge. The faucet itself is rarely finished. Most handle leaks can be fixed in under an hour with basic hand tools and one small part.

Name your handle type first, then buy the matching part. Guessing at a repair kit is how people end up with a drawer full of extras and a faucet that still weeps.

Which handle do you have?

Identify the type, the usual leak sign, and the part to check.

Gather this before you start

Hand tools

A small flat screwdriver, a Phillips driver, a hex key set, and an adjustable wrench. Wrap the wrench jaws in tape so they do not mar the finish.

Parts

O-ring assortment or the exact cartridge for your model, plus plumber's grease. Take the old part with you when you shop.

Cleanup

A towel for the sink bottom, a cup for vinegar, and a small tray to hold screws in order.

The repair in eight moves

  1. Close the supply

    Shut the hot and cold valves under the sink. If a valve will not close, stop and use the main shutoff.

  2. Plug the drain

    A rag in the drain keeps a dropped screw from vanishing.

  3. Pop the cap

    Pry the decorative cap on top of the handle with a small flat tool. Take care with the finish.

  4. Remove the screw

    Back out the handle screw, a Phillips or a hex type, and lift the handle straight off.

  5. Open the stem

    Unscrew the retaining nut or clip. Note how everything sits, and take a photo.

  6. Inspect the parts

    Look for flattened O-rings, cracked washers, or grit on the seat.

  7. Replace and grease

    Match the new O-rings or cartridge to the old. Coat rubber parts lightly with plumber's grease.

  8. Reassemble and test

    Reverse the order, open the faucet, then turn the supply valves back on slowly and watch the base.

Handle-specific notes

Compression handles

The stem is the moving part. If the packing nut is loose, a quarter turn may stop the leak. If it is soft and worn, replace the O-ring or packing string under the nut.

Cartridge and ceramic disc handles

Pull the whole cartridge and compare it with the new one before you reassemble. The cartridge must sit in the same position, or hot and cold will swap sides. Many cartridges are held by a clip or a nut, and the clip is easy to lose.

Ball-type handles

These have the most tiny pieces. Keep them in a tray in the order you removed them, and replace the springs and seals together, not one at a time.

Signs the handle is not the real problem

  • The leak keeps coming back within weeks, which points to a damaged seat or body.
  • The handle stays loose even after you tighten the nut, so the threads are stripped.
  • Water shows up in the cabinet but not at the handle, so the leak is a supply line or a drain.
  • The finish is flaking or the metal looks pitted.
  • Hot water scalds quickly because the cartridge no longer mixes correctly.

Any of these means a repair kit is a short-term patch. A faucet repair visit sorts out whether a part or the whole fixture needs work.

When to stop and call a licensed plumber

Do not force a handle that will not budge. Corroded screws and stuck stems snap, and a snapped stem can turn a small fix into a full replacement.

Call if the shutoff valves leak or will not turn, if the fixture is a shower valve behind a wall, or if you see water you cannot trace. A slow hidden leak also raises your bill, and a leak detection visit finds it without opening walls blindly.

Before you call: what to shut off and who to reach

Use the shutoff valves under the sink first, then the main shutoff in the house if they fail. Residents cannot open city water meters, so a meter-side shutoff is not yours to use.

If no valve in the house will stop the water, call a licensed plumber on the 24/7 line. For a problem on the street side, Public Works runs an after-hours water emergency line. Homes in Willard and Perry can reach the same dispatch.

Brigham City note: city meters, shutoffs, and pressure testing

Brigham City does not allow residents to access its water meters, so the shutoff you can use is inside your home. Learn where the main shutoff sits before a handle fails, because a stuck fixture valve leaves it as your only option. For street-side trouble, call Public Works at (435) 734-6615.
