Leak Detection Cost in Brigham City, UT: What Moves the Number

Leak detection cost in Brigham City, UT comes down to where the water is escaping, how hard it is to reach, and whether you want it found or found and fixed.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

The plain answer on leak detection cost

Leak detection is priced by the effort needed to locate the leak, not by the size of the house. A leak you can hear behind an accessible wall is a shorter job than a pinhole under a concrete slab. Detection-only visits and find-and-fix visits are also quoted differently.

This page gives no dollar amounts, since a number without your details would be a guess. It does show what shapes the quote, so you can describe the problem well on the call and hear a clear price before work starts.

Four questions that predict your quote

  1. Where does the water show up? A stain on a ceiling, a wet patch in the yard, and a bill that keeps climbing all point to different search areas.
  2. How many fixtures and lines are in play? One bathroom is a smaller search than a whole house with a basement and an outdoor line.
  3. How hard is the pipe to reach? Open basement ceilings are easy. Concrete, finished walls, and buried lines take more care.
  4. Do you want the leak found, or found and repaired? A repair adds parts, labor, and sometimes opening a wall.

Answer these four before you call and the quote you hear will be much closer to the final one.

Detection only, or find and fix

Detection only

You pay to locate the leak and mark it. You can then take the finding to any trade for the repair. This suits an insurance claim or a landlord report.

Find and fix

The plumber locates the leak and repairs it in the same visit when access allows. The quote may be set in two steps, because the repair cost is unknown until the leak is found.

Locate then decide

You choose after the finding. A pinhole in an accessible copper line is a spot repair. A failing line in a slab may point to rerouting the pipes.

If a claim, a sale, or a landlord needs paperwork, ask for the finding in writing. It should name the leak location, describe the cause, and include photos where possible. That record is often worth more than the repair itself when someone else has to approve the cost.

What a detection visit usually looks like

  1. Interview

    The plumber asks where you see signs, when the bill changed, and what has been repaired before.

  2. Isolate

    Sections of the house are closed off one at a time, which shows whether the loss is inside or outside.

  3. Pinpoint

    Listening gear, a moisture meter, and thermal imaging narrow the spot. A pressure test confirms which line is holding.

  4. Report

    You get the location, the likely cause, and the repair options, with the price explained before any work starts.

A no-cost first pass you can do yourself

Before you book, shut off every tap and appliance. Listen for a hissing sound near the water heater, the main shutoff, and the laundry area. Check that no toilet is running, because a silent flapper leak can waste a lot of water.

A dye tablet or a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank will show a flapper leak if color reaches the bowl within 15 minutes without a flush. Ruling out a toilet can save you a detection visit.

Red flags in a leak detection quote

  • A single number given with no questions about where the leak is.
  • No mention of how the leak will be pinpointed, only a promise that it will be found.
  • A repair price quoted before the leak is located.
  • No clear statement on whether surfaces will be opened, and who restores them.
  • A refusal to say what the visit covers if nothing is found.

Insurance and the water bill

Homeowners policies often cover sudden accidental water damage, but not wear, neglect, or the failed part itself. Detection may be covered in some policies and not in others, so check yours before you book. Keep the written finding and any photos.

A rising bill is a clue, not proof. Ask Public Works what the meter shows, since residents cannot open the city's meters themselves. A leak detection service can then target the right part of the house.

When it cannot wait

If water is spraying, pooling near the electrical panel, or soaking a ceiling, shut off the main and call the 24/7 line. A broken line needs burst pipe repair first, and detection can follow once the water is off.

Local note

Brigham City note: meters, pressure, and before-you-dig calls

Brigham City does not let residents access its water meters, so ask Public Works at (435) 734-6615 about the meter side of a suspected leak. That tells you whether to look inside or at the line to the street.

The city also offers water pressure testing. Knowing your pressure helps separate a leak from a pressure problem. If detection points to a buried line, Blue Stakes of Utah wants a call to 811 before excavation, so build that wait into your repair timeline.
