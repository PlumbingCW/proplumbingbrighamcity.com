Can You Replace Your Own Water Heater? A Brigham City, UT Reality Check

Replacing your own water heater in Brigham City, UT is possible for a skilled handy owner with an electric tank, but a gas unit carries risks that make most people hire it out.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 6 min read.

Straight answer: sometimes, and gas changes the math

An electric tank swap is the easier of the two. You shut off the breaker, drain the old tank, move the water lines, and wire the new one. A gas swap adds a gas connection, a vent that must pull exhaust out of the house, and a burner that has to light safely.

Plenty of owners have done both. The real question is whether you can test your own work, because a slow leak, a loose gas fitting, or a weak vent will not announce itself during the first hot shower.

Before you buy anything, read the permit and license sections below. They can decide the whole project.

If you smell gas, the order of operations matters

  • Leave the house first. Do not flip switches or hunt for the leak.
  • Once you are safely away, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line.
  • Do not relight, test, or restart the heater until the line has been checked.
  • Keep pets and neighbors out of the area until responders clear it.

Enbridge Gas Utah advises exactly this sequence: leave, then call. No water heater project is worth staying inside to finish.

Who is allowed to do the work, and what needs a permit

Permit rules vary, so ask the Brigham City building office at (435) 734-6604 before starting. The city's own page does not spell out a water heater exemption, and guessing wrong can cost you a failed inspection when you sell.

Ask three things on that call: whether a permit is required for a like-for-like swap, whether a homeowner may pull it, and which plumbing code edition applies. Utah adopts plumbing codes at the state level, and the office can tell you which edition is in force.

Also ask Public Works about thermal expansion. The city publishes guidance on how water heating systems handle expansion, and it affects whether the new install needs an expansion tank.

How to verify any plumber you hire

If you decide to hand it off, check the license yourself. The Utah Division of Professional Licensing runs a Verify a License tool at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html, and any homeowner can use it.

DOPL lists these plumber classes: apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master, and residential master. Look up the name and class, then confirm that the person who shows up matches the person who quoted.

You can then compare quotes on the same terms. A clear price before work starts, written down, beats a ballpark that grows on the day.

A fair way to decide

  1. Name the fuel

    Gas means a licensed plumber for at least the gas and vent connections.

  2. Call the building office

    Get the permit answer in writing or note the date and the name you spoke with.

  3. Inspect the setup

    Check where the old unit drains, how the vent runs, and whether an expansion tank or shutoff valve is missing.

  4. Count the hands

    A full tank is heavy and awkward. If you cannot get a second person, plan for professional help.

  5. Price your time

    Add up the parts, haul-away, and a weekend. Compare that to a water heater installation quote with a clear price before work starts.

Prep list if you go ahead anyway

  • A helper for the lift, and a hand truck rated for the weight of a full tank.
  • New flexible connectors and a new relief valve, not the old ones reused.
  • Fresh dielectric or approved fittings where copper meets steel.
  • A garden hose and a floor drain or a clear exit for tank water.
  • A plan for the old tank before the old tank is out of the closet.

Turn off the fuel and the water first, open a hot tap upstairs so the tank can drain, and let the water cool before you touch fittings. Scalding water from the drain valve is a real burn risk, and rushing this step is where most injuries happen.

What a failed DIY usually looks like

The common failures are boring. A union weeps under the tank for weeks. A relief valve discharge pipe is left uncapped or points the wrong way. A vent sags and spills exhaust into the closet.

None of these show up on day one. They show up as stained flooring, a rusty fitting, or a hot-water smell weeks later, which is why a second set of eyes from a licensed plumber before you close up is cheap insurance.

If the old tank is leaking now, shut off the water to it and read about emergency water heater repair before deciding.

Local note

Brigham City note: permits, gas, and license checks

The city's Community and Economic Development office at 20 N. Main, 2nd Floor, handles building permits and inspections at (435) 734-6604. Its page does not state a plumbing permit or water heater exemption, so a short phone call settles your project before you pull the old tank.

If your tank is gas, remember the safety order from the utility: leave first, then call 911 or the gas company. Homeowners in Perry or out in Tremonton should ask their own city office whether the same permit answers apply to their address.
