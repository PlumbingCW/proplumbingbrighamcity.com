How to Install a One-Piece Toilet in Brigham City, UT

Installing a one-piece toilet in Brigham City, UT is a manageable swap when the floor flange is sound and you have a second person to lift the bowl.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

Three green lights before you start

You can install a one-piece toilet yourself if three things are true. The floor flange is intact and sits at the finished floor, the floor around it is solid, and two people can lift a heavy fixture. One-piece models are often well over 50 pounds, and the porcelain chips easily.

If the flange is cracked, the floor is soft, or the old toilet was leaking from underneath, stop and bring in a licensed plumber. Those are repair jobs, and a new toilet set on a bad base will leak again.

Measure before you buy

Four measurements that decide which toilet fits.
MeasureHowWhy it matters
Rough-inFrom the finished wall behind the tank to the center of the floor boltsTwelve inches is the common distance. Ten and fourteen exist. A wrong match will not sit against the wall.
Bowl shapeRound or elongatedElongated is longer and needs more room in front.
Seat heightStandard or tallerTaller seats are easier on knees, and they matter for older adults.

Tools and parts to lay out first

  • New wax ring and closet bolts, if the toilet does not come with them.
  • A braided supply line sized to reach from the valve to the fill inlet.
  • Adjustable wrench, small level, plastic shims, and a putty knife.
  • Old towels, a sponge, a bucket, and a rag to plug the drain opening.
  • Disposable gloves, since the old wax and bowl are not pleasant.

Check the shutoff valve on the wall too. If it is stiff, weeping, or will not close all the way, do not trust it for the install. Replacing a shutoff means working on the supply pipe, which is a plumber's job unless you are confident with the fittings on your pipe.

Installation, in order

  1. Empty the old toilet

    Close the supply valve, flush, and sponge out the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line.

  2. Lift it off

    Remove the nuts on the floor bolts and lift straight up with a helper. Set it on towels.

  3. Scrape and inspect

    Remove the old wax from the flange. Check the flange for cracks, and cover the drain opening with a rag to block sewer gas.

  4. Set the bolts and ring

    Slide new closet bolts into the flange slots. Place a new wax ring on the flange, or on the horn of the bowl, as the maker directs.

  5. Lower the bowl

    Align the holes with the bolts and press down evenly. Do not rock the bowl, since that breaks the seal.

  6. Tighten gently

    Add washers and nuts, then snug each side in turns. Stop as soon as the bowl stops moving. Overtightening cracks porcelain.

  7. Connect the water

    Attach the supply line, open the valve slowly, and let the tank fill.

  8. Test the seal

    Flush several times, and check the base and the connections with dry paper.

Where one-piece installs go wrong

Cracked porcelain

The most expensive mistake. Tighten the bolts a little at a time, alternating sides, and never use a long wrench to force it.

Rocked wax seal

Moving the bowl after it touches the wax breaks the seal. If the bowl is off line, lift and reset it with a fresh ring.

Wobble

An uneven floor makes the bowl rock. Use plastic shims under the base, not extra bolt pressure.

Slow tank fill

Debris from the supply line can clog the fill valve. Flush the line into a bucket before you connect it.

When it is not a DIY job

  • The flange is cracked, rusted, or sits below the finished floor.
  • The floor around the toilet gives under your weight.
  • The drain pipe has moved or needs a new rough-in.
  • Water appears at the base after a flush, and a new ring did not fix it.
  • The toilet is slow to drain or gurgles, which points to a line problem.

A leak at the base of an installed toilet can rot the subfloor in silence. For a floor repair or flange fix, our toilet repair service handles the plumbing side. A relocated drain falls under plumbing installation, and permit rules vary.

After the install: the first week

Check the base for dampness after the first few flushes, then again the next morning. Keep a dry paper towel at the base overnight if you want a clear answer. A slow weep looks like a dry floor until the towel comes up wet.

Listen to the tank. It should fill and then go quiet. A constant hiss means the fill valve or the flapper needs adjusting. Crews in Tremonton, about 20 miles north, and Willard, about 7 miles south, can handle a flange or leak that the swap uncovered.

Local note

Brigham City note: cold garages and fill-valve scale

Brigham City's January lows average 18.1 F, based on NOAA climate normals. A toilet stored in an unheated garage can hold ice in the tank or bowl, so keep it indoors until install day, and drain the tank first.

The city's water page lists hardness at 17 to 20 grains per gallon. Scale builds up around fill valves and flush valves, so an easy cleaning habit helps. If the new tank fills slowly or whistles in a year or two, scale is a likely suspect. Ask Public Works for the current figure.
