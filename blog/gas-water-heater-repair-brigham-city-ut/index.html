Gas Water Heater Repair in Brigham City, UT: What Is Safe to Check

Gas water heater repair in Brigham City, UT starts with one rule: if you smell gas, leave the house before you do anything else.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

Safety first

Gas smell first: leave, then call

If you smell gas, hear hissing near the heater, or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, leave the building immediately. Do not flip switches, use a phone, or try to find the leak. Once you are safely outside and away, call 911 or your gas utility's 24-hour emergency line.

Enbridge Gas Utah lists its emergency line as 1-800-767-1689. Do not go back in to relight a pilot or shut a valve. Wait outside until responders say the house is clear. Repairs come after the all-clear.

With no gas smell, a homeowner can safely read the temperature setting, confirm the gas valve handle is open, and look for water around the tank. Anything that involves the burner, the gas control, or the vent belongs to a licensed plumber. The rest of this guide sorts out which is which.

The checks a homeowner can do without opening the unit

  1. Read the temperature setting on the control. Many homes use a setting near 120 F, and a dial pushed to the lowest or vacation setting can explain a cold shower.
  2. Check that the gas shutoff valve at the heater is open. The handle should line up with the pipe.
  3. Look at the pilot window if the heater has one, but only if there is no gas smell.
  4. Read the label. It gives the maker's lighting instructions, and you should follow those exactly.
  5. Look for puddles, rust streaks, or a wet floor under the tank.

If the pilot is out and there is no gas smell, one relight attempt per the label is reasonable. If it will not stay lit, stop. The part that holds the flame is a safety device, and a plumber should replace it.

Why a gas heater rumbles, pops, or runs out early

Minerals settle at the bottom of the tank and bake into a layer that the burner has to heat through. That causes popping, rumbling, and slower recovery. It also overheats the steel under the burner.

Hard water makes this worse. Flushing the tank can help, but it is not a casual task. Hot water can scald, the gas control must be set first, and a drain valve clogged with scale can snap if forced. Call a plumber if the valve will not flow.

Signs it is time to call a licensed plumber

  • The pilot goes out repeatedly, or the burner flame looks lazy and yellow.
  • The temperature and pressure relief valve drips or discharges. Never cap it.
  • Water pools around the base, or the tank shows rust at the seams.
  • The flue pipe is loose, disconnected, or stained near the joints.
  • The heater is far past the age where tanks tend to fail, and repairs keep stacking up.

Our gas water heater repair service covers the burner, control, and venting checks. If the tank itself has failed, a replacement is the honest answer, and you hear the reasoning first.

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

Parts like a thermocouple or a gas control are repairable. A tank that leaks through the steel is finished, because water has found a way out.

Repair makes sense when the tank is sound and the problem is a single part. Replace when the tank leaks, when rust shows at the seams, or when the same heater has needed repeated work. Ask the plumber to show you the failed part, so you can see why.

What to do while you wait for a plumber

  1. Check for gas

    If you smell gas at any point, leave and call 911 or the gas utility.

  2. Close the cold supply

    If the tank is leaking, shut the cold water valve on top of the heater.

  3. Turn the control off

    Set the gas control to the off position if it is safe to reach and there is no gas smell.

  4. Protect the floor

    Lay down towels and move stored items out of the way.

  5. Keep notes

    Write down the model and the symptoms for dispatch. Crews reach Perry and Tremonton as well as Brigham City.

Local note

Brigham City note: gas safety and hard water scale

Enbridge Gas Utah's safety guidance is the same everywhere in town: if you smell gas, leave immediately, and call once you are safely away. Memorize the steps now. Keep the utility number somewhere you can reach from outside.

The Brigham City Water Division lists hardness at 17 to 20 grains per gallon, which is very hard by the USGS scale. Scale builds on tank bottoms and heating surfaces, which shortens heater life. The city also publishes guidance on thermal expansion in water heating systems, so ask Public Works before you add an expansion tank.
