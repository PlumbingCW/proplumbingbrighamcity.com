Sewer Camera Inspection Cost in Brigham City, UT: What Drives the Price

Sewer camera inspection cost in Brigham City, UT depends on a handful of things you can see before anyone arrives, and none of them is a mystery.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

What you are really paying for

A sewer camera inspection is a recorded look inside your main sewer line using a camera on a flexible cable. The price follows the work involved, not a fixed menu. Five things move it most: how easy the line is to enter, how long it runs, whether it must be cleaned first, whether the line needs to be located, and what documentation you want.

This page gives no dollar amounts, because a price quoted without knowing your cleanout would be a guess. It does tell you what to report on the phone so the number you hear is firm. Our sewer camera inspection service works that way, with a clear price before work starts.

Three reasons people book, and how each shapes the visit

Buying a house

The goal is a recorded condition report before closing. You want the whole run to the street, the video, and notes on roots, offsets, and cracks.

Repeated backups

The goal is the cause. Expect cleaning first, then the camera, because grease, roots, and a sagging section look different on video.

Planning a project

The goal is the path and depth. Locating the line matters more than the video if you are putting in a patio, a fence, or an addition.

When a cleaning comes before the camera

If the line is clogged, the camera stops at the clog and shows you nothing beyond it. That is why a quote often has two parts, clearing the line and then recording it. A sewer line cleaning pass opens the pipe so the lens can travel the full run.

Ask whether the quote covers the camera alone or the camera plus cleaning. A low number for the camera alone sounds fine until the crew finds a blockage and the job changes. Knowing the split up front keeps the surprise out of it.

Why two quotes for the same house can differ

Two shops can describe the same visit in different words. One quote may include cleaning, a locate, and a written report. Another may cover a short look from the cleanout and nothing else.

Line the quotes up item by item. Ask each shop what happens if the camera hits a blockage at 30 feet, and whether the video runs to the street. The lower number is only the better deal if it covers the same work.

Also ask whether the visit ends with a recommendation. A video with no interpretation leaves you holding footage you cannot read.

What you should walk away with

  • A video of the full run, not a clip of the first few feet.
  • Notes on what was seen, such as roots, cracks, sags, or a belly that holds water.
  • The distance to each problem, measured from the cleanout.
  • A plain recommendation: leave it alone, clean it on a schedule, or repair a section.
  • A mark on the ground if the line was located.

If a visit leaves out the video, the notes, or a recommendation, you paid for less than the job was worth.

Questions worth asking before you book

  1. Does the quote include cleaning if the line is blocked?
  2. Will I get the video and notes after the visit?
  3. Can you find the line from above ground if I need it marked?
  4. If a repair is needed, will you say so before any digging price is discussed?
  5. Who is licensed on the job? You can look any plumber up with the state's license lookup.
