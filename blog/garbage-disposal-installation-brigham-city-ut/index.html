Garbage Disposal Installation in Brigham City, UT: What the Job Involves

Garbage disposal installation in Brigham City, UT is a realistic weekend job when you are swapping an existing unit, and a plumber's job when the setup is new.

By Brigham City Pro Plumbing. Published October 29, 2026. Updated October 29, 2026. 7 min read.

Can you install a disposal yourself? Usually, with two conditions

Yes, if you are replacing a disposal that is already there and you have a switched outlet under the sink. A like-for-like swap uses the same drain opening, the same sink flange, and the same power. Plan on an afternoon and a helper to hold the unit while you lock it in place.

Hand it off if the sink has never had a disposal, if there is no outlet or switch, or if the drain below is slow. The first case needs a drain connection and often an electrical change. The second and third are the situations where a careful DIY job still ends in a leak or a clog.

The installation in eight steps

  1. Cut the power

    Unplug the old unit, or turn off its breaker and test with a non-contact tester. Do not trust the wall switch alone.

  2. Disconnect the old unit

    Loosen the drain tube and the dishwasher hose. Hold the disposal while you twist the mounting ring free, since it is heavy.

  3. Clean the sink opening

    Scrape off old putty. Check the flange for cracks or a warped lip, and replace it if it is damaged.

  4. Seat the new flange

    Press a rope of plumber's putty under the flange and tighten the mounting ring until putty squeezes out evenly. Wipe the excess.

  5. Knock out the dishwasher plug

    If a dishwasher will connect, tap out the plug inside the side inlet and fish it out. Skipping this blocks the dishwasher drain.

  6. Hang the disposal

    Lift it onto the mounting ring and rotate until it locks. Align the discharge outlet with the trap arm.

  7. Connect drain and hose

    Fit the discharge tube with its gasket, then clamp the dishwasher hose to the side inlet.

  8. Restore power and test

    Plug in, turn on the water, run the unit a few seconds, and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

Mistakes that cause leaks, jams, and hum

  • The dishwasher plug stays in place, so dishwasher water has nowhere to go and backs up.
  • One side of the mounting ring gets overtightened, which tilts the unit and weeps at the flange.
  • A flattened old discharge gasket is reused. New units ship with one, so use it.
  • The unit runs before the joints are checked, so a drip soaks the cabinet for an hour.
  • Cold water is left off during use. A steady flow of cold water carries scraps through the trap and keeps grease from setting.
  • The plumber's putty is skipped or smeared thin, and the sink flange seeps into the cabinet below.

Power and wiring: where DIY stops

A disposal draws real current, so treat the wiring with respect. Switch off the breaker, confirm the circuit is dead, and keep the cord clear of the damp cabinet floor.

If your unit is hardwired rather than plugged in, or if you have to add an outlet, bring in a licensed electrician. A plumber handles the water side and the drain. Permit rules for electrical and plumbing changes vary, so ask the building office before you start.

Continuous feed or batch feed, and how big

Continuous feed

Runs while the wall switch is on. It is the common type and the easier one to live with day to day.

Batch feed

Runs only when a lid is twisted into place. It costs more to buy, and it suits homes with small children.

Motor size

A bigger motor grinds faster and jams less, though it needs more room under the sink. Measure the cabinet before you shop.

Whatever you choose, a disposal does not make a drain bulletproof. Grease, fibrous peels, and coffee grounds still build up in the trap and the line beyond it. A drain cleaning visit is the fix when the sink backs up even with the unit running.

What a licensed plumber adds to the job

A plumber installs the unit and checks everything it touches. That means the drain tube slope, the trap, the vent that lets the sink drain, and the shutoff valves you will need next time.
