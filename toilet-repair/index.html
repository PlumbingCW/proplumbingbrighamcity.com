Toilet overflowing or leaking at the base: do this first

A toilet that spills over, rocks on its base or leaves water across the bathroom floor cannot wait for next week, and our line for toilet repair in Brigham City, UT answers 24/7. Toilet repair means fixing or replacing the parts that fill, flush, seal and drain the fixture, from the fill valve inside the tank to the wax ring under the bowl.

While you wait, turn the small oval-handled valve on the wall clockwise until it stops. Lift the tank lid and hold the float up if water is still rising, then lay towels around the base. Keep everyone off wet tile, and leave the bowl alone if the water looks dark or smells like sewage.

Diagnosis

Read the symptoms before you buy parts

Most people describe the trouble long before they know a part name. The toilet runs by itself, hisses after a flush, gurgles when the shower drains, or leaves a damp ring on the floor. Match what you see to the table, then expect us to confirm it on site.

Running toilets, wax rings and fill valves

A running toilet is the quiet one that hides in the water bill. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and the fix is usually small.

Fill valve

If the tank keeps refilling or never shuts off, the fill valve or float is worn. We replace the whole assembly so the water level and shutoff are set correctly.

Wax ring

A seep around the base means the seal under the bowl has let go. The toilet comes up, the flange is checked, and a new ring goes down before it is set back and bolted.

Supply valve

The stop valve on the wall is the one you will reach for in a flood. If it is stuck, weeping or will not close, we swap it so the next problem is easy to contain.

Handle and flapper chain

A broken lever or a chain with too much slack keeps the flapper from lifting or seating. It is a short repair, and it is the cheapest cause on this list to fix.

When plunging stops working: clogs deep in the line

A closet auger reaches the trap just past the bowl and solves most single-toilet clogs. If it comes back clean and the toilet still struggles, the blockage sits deeper. Two toilets that will not flush at once, or a floor drain that bubbles when you flush, point to the main line.

That is when a cable or camera run through the cleanout makes sense, and our drain cleaning crew handles it. Roots and heavy buildup in the house-to-street run call for main sewer cleaning instead. Stop flushing until it is cleared, because every flush pushes more water toward the lowest drain in the house.

Before shopping for a new bowl, measure from the wall to the closet bolts. Twelve inches is the common rough-in, and round and elongated bowls both fit that spacing. A one-piece toilet has no tank-to-bowl seam to leak, but it is heavy, so plan on two people to set it.

We install the toilet you bought or help you pick one, and larger fixture changes can be paired with new plumbing installation. Mention on the phone that you want the old unit removed so that work is part of the price we discuss up front.

What shapes the price of a toilet job

Part or whole fixture

A flapper or fill valve is a different job from a new toilet. The first is parts and a short visit, the second adds the fixture and setting it.

Flange and floor condition

A damaged flange or soft subfloor under the toilet adds repair work that cannot be seen until the bowl is up.

Clog depth

A trap clog is quick. A blockage in the branch or main line needs more equipment and time.

Time of day

Night and weekend dispatch is priced differently from a booked daytime visit, and we tell you before work starts.

Access

A tight bathroom, a second-floor unit or a basement ejector setup changes how long the work takes.

Is it really the toilet?

Some water on the bathroom floor comes from elsewhere. A drip under the sink points to faucet and supply line repair, not the bowl. Sewage rising in a tub or shower while the toilet is fine means the main line is blocked.

If water has reached a wall outlet or a light fixture, shut off that breaker only if you can reach the panel dry, and call an electrician. If you smell gas, leave the house first and call 911 or the gas utility. Neither is a toilet repair, and neither should wait for us.
